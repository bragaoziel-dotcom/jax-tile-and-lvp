Why backsplashes deserve their own page

A backsplash is 20 to 40 square feet that sits at eye level, wraps around outlets, corners and a range, and finishes — or dates — the whole kitchen. Per square foot it is the most cut-intensive tile work in a home. That is exactly why it is quoted after a measure instead of off a rate card: two kitchens with the same wall area can need very different amounts of cutting.

What a backsplash scope includes

  • Removal of the old backsplash or prep of the bare wall, as the measure finds it
  • Layout planning — where the pattern starts, how it turns corners, how it meets the cabinets
  • Setting with outlet and switch cuts fitted, not notched around
  • Grout, sealing after cure, and caulked transitions at the counter

Finished tile work is on the projects page. Full kitchen remodeling beyond the tile scope is on bragaremodeling.com.

Tile choices: subway, mosaic, handmade-look

Ceramic subway tile is the default for a reason: it suits almost any cabinet, and a stacked or offset pattern changes the feel without changing the budget category. Mosaics add texture and are the usual pick behind a range. Handmade-look tile — with its slight surface variation — hides the small imperfections of real kitchen walls better than a perfectly flat glossy tile does. The body is almost always ceramic or glazed porcelain; on a dry wall, style leads and the measure confirms the fit.

Layout is the craft

The difference between a backsplash that looks installed and one that looks designed is decided before the first tile: where the first row starts so the top row does not end in a sliver at the cabinet, how the pattern turns the corner, and whether an outlet lands mid-tile (a clean cut) or on a joint (a visible one). We plan it at the measure and the written quote reflects the real cutting involved.

Pairing with a floor

Many backsplash requests arrive with a kitchen floor in the same breath. The floor is often the LVP package at its published price — waterproof plank handles kitchen water better than the old sheet vinyl it replaces — while the backsplash is quoted as its own job. One measure, two line items, no surprises in either.

Backsplash questions

Backsplash installation — FAQs

How much does a backsplash cost to install?

There is no published per-square-foot price for backsplash tile. It is quoted after a free measure because the price depends on the tile, the wall area, and how many outlet and corner cuts the layout needs. A backsplash is small in area, so the quote is a single written job price, not a package rate.

What tile works best for a kitchen backsplash?

Ceramic is the standard backsplash material — it is a dry wall application, so the huge style range of ceramic and glazed tile is open to you, from subway to handmade-look. Porcelain works too. What matters more than the body is the layout plan around outlets and cabinets, which happens at the measure.

Can you tile a backsplash over my existing one?

Rarely well. An old backsplash adds thickness at outlets and edges and its bond is unknown. Removing it and starting from a flat wall gives clean edges at the counter and cabinets. We look at what is there at the measure and write the honest scope.

How long does a backsplash take?

Most backsplashes are measured in days, not weeks: set one day, grout and finish after cure. The written quote lists the work days before anything starts.

Do I need to seal a backsplash?

The tile itself is usually glazed and does not need sealing; the grout does. Grout is sealed after it cures, and resealing behind a range — where grease and steam concentrate — is normal maintenance every few years.

Can a backsplash be part of a bigger kitchen floor job?

Yes. Homeowners often pair a backsplash with the LVP package in the same kitchen — the floor at the published package price and the backsplash as its own written quote. One measure covers both.
