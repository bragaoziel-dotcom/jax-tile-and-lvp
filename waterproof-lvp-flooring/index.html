Anatomy of a waterproof plank

A modern LVP plank is layers: a wear layer on top that takes the traffic, a printed design layer under it, and a core that decides how the plank behaves. In rigid-core products the core is SPC — stone-plastic composite — a dense, stable board that does not absorb water and does not flex like the older flexible vinyl. That rigidity is the real product: it keeps joints closed, resists denting from furniture, and tolerates the temperature swings of a Florida slab better than a soft plank does.

What waterproof does not mean

Honesty section, because this word gets abused. Waterproof describes the plank, not the room. A mop bucket, a pet accident, a day of wet beach feet — all fine, they stay on the surface. A slow plumbing leak under the floor or water pushed through seams during a flood is a subfloor problem, and it will damage whatever floor sits on top of it. We say this at the measure too: if the subfloor is wet from a leak, the leak gets fixed first, then the floor goes down.

SPC rigid core vs flexible vinyl vs laminate

Rigid core (SPC) is dense and stable; it bridges minor surface imperfections and clicks together into a floating floor. Flexible vinyl — older sheet and glue-down products — conforms to the floor under it, which makes prep everything, and it is more common in commercial work now. Laminate looks similar on a shelf but its core is fiberboard: water in the joints swells it, permanently. In kitchens, laundry rooms and beach homes, that single difference decides the floor.

Waterproof & rigid-core LVP — FAQs

What does "waterproof" actually mean on an LVP floor?

It describes the plank: the wear surface and core do not absorb water, so spills, pet accidents and wet footprints sit on top instead of soaking in. It does not mean a room can flood without consequence — water that reaches the subfloor through seams or from a plumbing leak underneath is a different problem, and no floor finish fixes an active leak.

What is SPC rigid core, and is it part of the package?

SPC is stone-plastic composite — a rigid core that makes the plank denser and more dimensionally stable than flexible vinyl, and more forgiving over minor subfloor imperfections. Rigid-core construction is part of what 'waterproof LVP' means on this site, and the homepage describes SPC rigid core flooring among the things this company knows and installs. The exact thickness and wear layer of the current in-stock lot are confirmed on your written quote at the free measure, because the lot changes.

Waterproof vs water-resistant — what is the difference?

Water-resistant products tolerate surface moisture for a limited time before it soaks in; a waterproof plank's core does not absorb it. Laminate is the common comparison: its fiberboard core swells when water gets into the joints. LVP's vinyl core has nothing to swell, which is why it works in kitchens, laundry rooms and beach entries where laminate fails.

Can waterproof LVP go in a bathroom or laundry room?

Yes — those are two of its best rooms. The subfloor must be dry and sound first, transitions get sealed where water is likely, and the toilet reset, when needed, is a quoted extra ($99). A waterproof floor surface is not a fix for a leaking supply line under it.

Will a rigid-core floor feel hard or cold underfoot?

Rigid core is firmer underfoot than carpet or a cushioned sheet vinyl — that density is what makes it stable. On a Jacksonville slab it feels like other hard floors: cool in summer, and area rugs do the warming in winter. Attached-pad products soften footfall sound; what your in-stock options include is confirmed at the measure.
