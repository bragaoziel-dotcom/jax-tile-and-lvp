A shower is a system, not a surface

Every shower failure story starts the same way: tile and grout treated as if they were the waterproofing. They are not — grout is porous and tile is a finish. The waterproof layer behind the tile is what keeps a shower from rotting the wall and floor structure over years of daily use. That is why this page exists separately from general tile: the scope conversation, the prep and the quote are different for wet work.

The floor: slope and small tile

A shower floor has to drain, which means it is built on a slope — and large flat tile cannot follow a slope without lippage and puddles. Small-format mosaic tile bends with the pan and its grout lines give footing. That is a construction fact, not a style preference, and it is why shower floors and shower walls are usually two different tiles in the same palette.

Walls, curbs and niches: layout first

On the walls, larger formats mean fewer grout lines and easier cleaning; the layout is planned so cuts land at corners and the niche lines up with the grout pattern instead of cutting through it. Curbs get the same tile logic as the floor they contain. All of it is drawn into the written scope before setting starts — the quote is where waterproofing, tile formats and any warranty are confirmed, in writing.

Tub-shower conversions

The most common Jacksonville shower job is removing a one-piece fiberglass tub-shower — standard in 1980s and builder homes — and tiling the space. The decisions are made at the measure: tub kept and re-tiled around, or a walk-in shower; the valve and drain positions stay or move (plumbing changes are part of the broader remodeling scope on bragaremodeling.com). What this crew quotes and sets is the tile system: prep, waterproofing as scoped, tile, grout and seal.

Time and cure days

Showers cannot be rushed past chemistry: mortar cures, grout cures, sealer cures. The written quote lists the work days and the date the shower returns to use — plan on the bathroom being out of service for the duration, which is why many homeowners pair a shower job with the bathroom floor while the room is already apart.

Shower tile questions

Shower tile — FAQs

How much does a tile shower cost?

There is no published per-square-foot price for shower tile. The quote is written after a free measure because it depends on the shower size, the tile format, demolition of the old surround, and the waterproofing scope. The $3.99 per square foot figure on this website is only the waterproof LVP package for floors.

Do shower projects include waterproofing?

A shower is not treated like a dry wall. Waterproofing is discussed at the measure and included only when it is part of the agreed tile scope. The specific system and any written warranty are confirmed on the quote before work starts.

What tile goes on a shower floor?

Small-format tile — mosaics on a mesh sheet are standard — because small pieces follow the slope to the drain and the many grout lines give footing on a wet floor. Large-format tile belongs on the walls, where it means fewer grout lines to maintain.

Can you convert my fiberglass tub-shower to a tiled shower?

That conversion is one of the most common shower jobs: the fiberglass unit comes out, the walls and floor pan area are prepped and waterproofed as part of the agreed scope, and tile goes in. Whether a tub stays or the shower becomes walk-in is decided with you at the measure and written into the quote.

How long does a tile shower take?

Longer than a floor, because the work has cure steps: setting, grouting and sealing happen on separate days. The written quote lists the work days and the date the shower can be used again.

What grout and sealing does a shower need?

Joint width decides the grout type — narrow joints get unsanded, wider joints get sanded. Grout is sealed after it cures, and resealing is normal maintenance every few years in a shower. We cover the care routine when the job is done.
